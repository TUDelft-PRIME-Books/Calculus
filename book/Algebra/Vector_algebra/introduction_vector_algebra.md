# Vector algebra

This chapter introduces the algebra and geometry of vectors, providing a powerful language for describing and analysing quantities that possess both magnitude and direction. Beginning with vectors in the plane, we develop the fundamental operations that govern their behaviour and extend these ideas to higher-dimensional spaces. We then introduce the dot product and cross product, which allow us to measure lengths, angles, orthogonality, areas, and orientations using algebraic methods. Building on these tools, we show how vectors can be used to describe lines and planes, transforming geometric problems into problems of calculation and analysis. Finally, we introduce determinants through their geometric interpretation as signed areas and volumes, revealing their connections to orientation, linear independence, and invertibility. Together, these concepts provide a unified framework for understanding geometry through algebra and form an essential foundation for subsequent developments in linear algebra and multivariable mathematics.
